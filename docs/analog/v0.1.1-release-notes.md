## 1. Board receipts for five corners (SC-003 of the epic)

The word "acceptance" for these receipts means SC-003 of the epic: for each of the five corners the inverter's board receipt reads PASS. It is not the external team's 13-block acceptance table (part 4), and it is not a new physical measurement. A PASS line says the board's output equals the spec's fixed-point run of the same tables, bit for bit. The spec's distance to ngspice is the one its own tests state (#11123, #11214).

At the v0.1.0 tag the board had a receipt for tt only; the other four corners had tables and ngspice checks and no bitstream. That statement is replaced by this table. Build B is the form of the tt receipt: the corner's tables and the corner's own samples are in the RAM, and the board's own verdict is PASS on every well-formed line.

| corner | receipt lines | PASS | FAIL | S at N = 18F, three rounds (the corner's pin) | LUT (yosys) | nextpnr seed | Fmax after routing | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tt | 1217 | 1217 | 0 | `0x215DC5EAC4C48576` | 1426 | default | 103.61 MHz | #11220, at the v0.1.0 tag |
| ss | 1216 | 1216 | 0 | `0x987F22AC4132345B` | 1418 | default | 101.70 MHz | #12050, PR #12063 |
| ff | 1218 | 1218 | 0 | `0x836220E2C48AB136` | 1420 | default | 108.26 MHz | #12050, PR #12063 |
| fs | 1219 | 1219 | 0 | `0xA45CC48E565AD2EB` | 1417 | 3 | 104.18 MHz | #12050, PR #12063 |
| sf | 1217 | 1217 | 0 | `0x67F02DF65E28F63E` | 1415 | 2 | 116.73 MHz | #12050, PR #12063 |

All four new builds: 1219 FF, 0 DSP48E1, 4 RAMB36E1 + 1 RAMB18E1 (the four 625-word tables and the 400-word run). The corner is chosen by one literal, `BOARD_CORNER` in `specs/analog/mos_psp103_data.t27` (TT = 0, FF = 1, SS = 2, FS = 3, SF = 4); the committed value is 0. The sources of PR #12031 at head `750b3dc707` were used with only that literal changed.

Build A (PR #12031) loaded each corner's tables with the tt samples still in the RAM, so its PASS and FAIL split is the prediction (for ss 77 PASS and 323 FAIL a round) and the Y column carries the corner's own sample; it is the form of the ss control and not a pass receipt. In every Build A capture the board's S at N = 18F equals the pin that `mos_psp103.t27` stated before any board run.

What the receipts do not say:

- Each corner ran once. The fs corner needed more than one placement (seed 2 gave 96.42 MHz and seed 4 gave 93.55 MHz, both fail at 100 MHz; seed 3 gave 104.18 MHz and was loaded). Fmax is nextpnr's post-route estimate for one placement of one netlist, not a property of the circuit and not a speed claim.
- The judge was `grep` only: the count of well-formed lines, of PASS and FAIL suffixes, and the N = 18F lines with their S column. Not checked: that the three rounds are byte-equal column by column, and the L column for gaps.
- One gate waveform (two levels, an ideal step) and one load (500 fF, dt = 100 ps); no repetition, no temperature or voltage variation.

## 2. Mutation-test and test-strength changes

Question asked: do the tests of the analog specs notice a wrong line? `tri mutate spec` plants one single-line fault at a time (a mutant). Killed means a test failed; survived means every test passed; hung means the mutant ran into an endless loop; unviable means it did not compile. The census is the run on the unmodified spec; the gate is the run after the tests were appended, and it exits 0 only when every not-killed mutant is listed in the spec's accepted-mutants file `docs/analog/mutation-accepted-<spec>.txt`. In that file "accepted" counts the survivors and the hung mutants together, and every entry carries a class: equivalent (no test can tell), dead line (no input reaches it), hung, not constructed (a witness input is still needed), or undecided. An accepted survivor is therefore not a proof that its line is right. The census covers 17 of 17 analog specs (`docs/analog/mutation-census-rest-2026-10-25.md`, PR #12501, refresh PR #12552); the figures are per spec and are not added up.

Two small specs were censused and not gated: sar_trace_raw 42 mutants (38 / 1 / 3 / 0) and bench 58 (55 / 3 / 0 / 0). SC-002 of #12116 (a gate on every spec) is therefore not met to the letter for those two; #12116 was closed on 2026-10-27 by its default with that deviation recorded in the closing comment. The other specs are in the census note.

Other test-strength results:

- Seal refusal (#12065, `docs/analog/seal-refusal-2026-10-25.md`): with one planted single-line fault in each of 16 milestone specs, the bare `t27c seal <spec>` refuses 0 of 16 (it runs no tests), `t27c seal --save` refuses 16 of 16, and `--verify` refuses 16 of 16.
- Two load-constant faults (`SL_DC_LOAD_R` and `SL_TG_IN_LOAD`) give no signal in any test; they stay as documented survivors by decision (c) of #12069 (PR #12942, #12067).
- Strict coverage of the analog specs went from 5 gaps (145 of 150) to 0 gaps (150 of 150) (#12041, PR #12046).
- 151 asserts that compared a constant table element were folded by the compiler into no runtime assert; they now read through variables (13 specs, seals re-saved; #12166, PR #12191).
- Tool changes that are not analog specs: the first-pass clock spec (PR #12346, wired in PR #12625 for #12403), the compile-answer rule (PR #12636 for #12539), and the `STALE-T27C?` message of `tri coverage` (PR #12072).

## 4. What did not change

- Acceptance of the external team's per-block checks, as in v0.1.0: 9 of 13 blocks PASS (clock_gen, SAR_logic, integration, nand, nor, tgate, dynamic_comparator, inverter, tracking_switch), 0 FAIL, 4 NOT MODELLED (dff, DAC_control, DAC_control_top, neg_bottom_plate_logic). Reported separately, the 30 rows are 26 PASS, 0 FAIL and 4 NOT MODELLED. The counts are pinned by the test `the_thirteen_blocks_count_9_pass_0_fail_and_4_not_modelled_and_their_30_rows_26_pass_0_fail_and_4_not_modelled` in `specs/analog/accept_sar.t27`; the table is `docs/analog/accept-sar-2026-10-20.md`.
- No overall completion percentage is given: there is no defined aggregate measure, and the 9 of 13 blocks and the 26 of 30 rows are two different counts.
- The reference chain is not pinned end to end: the receipts part of #12027 and the three gate-level blocks of #12028 are not started (they need a machine with the reference toolchain and the golden deck).
- The full converter is not complete. `sar_adc.t27` still takes fitted numbers; the comparator and the switch at PSP103 level are not inside the converter loop (v0.1.0 notes, "What it does not claim").
- The four NOT MODELLED blocks are not modelled.
- The comparator step on the board (phase 2b, #11380) is not built; the phase 2a unit failed 100 MHz after routing (59.46 MHz) and was never loaded.
- Speed: the recorded v0.1 slowdown stands, 170000 to 196000 times slower than real time (#11313). This release adds no speed claim and no power claim.
- Verilog-A input is not a v0.1 capability; M7a (#12989) is research.

## Reproduce

`t27c test-report specs/analog/<spec>.t27` for a spec's tests (vacuous passes must be 0). The mutation runs are `tri mutate spec` with the per-spec accepted-mutants file; they take tens of minutes for the large specs and belong on a lab pane, not on a laptop. A corner's bitstream is the board top `specs/fpga/psp103_inv_ax7203.t27` with the literal `BOARD_CORNER` in `specs/analog/mos_psp103_data.t27` set to the corner's number; the exact build lines are in `docs/analog/board-psp103-inv-buildb-2026-10-24.md`.
